The brief, in short

This is my summary of the task, not the original. The company has been changed, and the inputs are summarised, not reproduced.

The setup

You are a PM at a research agency that runs consumer studies for brand insights teams. Four raw inputs have landed on your desk this week. Work out what is actually going on, decide what (if anything) to build, and write it up.

  • A customer discovery call. An insights director with a shrinking team explains where her time and credibility go.
  • Usage data and feature requests. Where hours and calendar days go in a study, how reports get used, what customers ask for, and how that splits by account size.
  • A competitive note. A cheaper self-serve rival, a fast low-cost shop that sells to a different buyer, and big customers building their own tools.
  • An internal Slack thread. Sales, the CTO, delivery, design and CS each push their own priority, and the CEO adds a retention target and a positioning question.

There is a hard constraint: capacity for one substantial build and one small one this quarter.

The ask

A 1–2 page memo, presentation or PRD, in about 3 hours, then a live defence of about 45 minutes. Cover:

  1. The problems, and how you extracted and classified them
  2. A ranked list of options, and how you ranked them
  3. One item, developed as a PRD-style solution
  4. How any LLM parts would work
  5. AI quality and risk: what could go wrong, and how you keep it trustworthy
  6. How you would measure success
  7. Open questions, and how you would answer them

Plus one paragraph on how you used AI: where it helped, where you did not trust it, and what you changed.

What they scored

Judgment under ambiguity, not polish. A well-argued call they disagree with beats a hedged one they agree with.
